# Evaluation of contract-graph-linter

Generated by `evaluation/evaluate_linter.py`. Run it again to regenerate this file.

## What was tested

- **Graphs.** 143 reference clause graphs from the public ContractGraphEval benchmark, and 3 contract graphs assembled from them (73 nodes and 104 edges, 230 nodes and 401 edges, 35 nodes and 41 edges). The contracts are commercial agreements from CUAD.
- **Planted defects.** One change at a time is made to a graph, with a known expected finding. A defect counts as detected when the expected check is reported on the changed node or edge and was not reported before the change.
- **Controls.** Changes that should not produce a finding: rewriting the graph without changing what it says, or adding something well-formed.
- **Unusable input.** Output that is not a usable graph. It must be reported or rejected, never passed.
- Clause-level defects are planted once in every clause graph. Contract-level defects are planted 40 times in each contract graph, at random places (seed 0).

## Two defects at once

Two different contract-level defects were planted in the same graph, to see whether one hides the other. Both expected findings appeared in 274 of 274 trials. Trials in which the second change altered the first planted defect itself were left out.

## Limits of this evaluation

- The graphs come from 3 contracts. The planted defects are placed in many positions, but the underlying documents are few.
- Planted defects are constructed. Detecting them shows that each check does what it is specified to do. It does not measure how often such defects occur in practice, or how useful a finding is to a reviewer.
- The reference graphs were labelled by a language model. Findings on the unmodified graphs are not counted here as right or wrong.
- Checks that compare the graph with the text only test whether a name appears in the clause, not whether the relation drawn from it is correct.
